How Cross-Border Capital Actually Travels:
Multi-Hop Correspondent Networks & ML Route Selection

Why do payment hops exist? Global financial institutions do not maintain direct account relationships with every bank worldwide. Instead, cross-border transactions rely on bilateral accounting ledgers (Nostro/Vostro accounts) and domestic clearing systems (e.g. Fedwire, CHAPS, RTGS). When a bank in the US transfers funds to a bank in India without a shared bilateral ledger, the transaction must traverse an intermediary correspondent banking chain.

The Operational Problem: Each hop introduces variable latency due to batch-window cutoffs, liquidity availability, currency spreads, and regulatory screening. Traditional routing uses rigid static lookup tables, leading to settlement bottlenecks, weekend delays, and unexpected counterparty rejections.

What this system does: Evaluates candidate correspondent corridors in real-time using Dual-Task Gradient Boosted ML (simultaneously predicting completion probability & settlement seconds), feeding an automated multi-objective decision engine to select the optimal route under strict SLA constraints.
